This setup revolves around making smartphones from a single key input, the microprocessor, with PrecisionParts supplying that component into the Production Center and finished smartphones then moving on to the Warehouse. Each smartphone requires 1 unit of microprocessor, and the plant starts with 4,304,466 units of smartphone inventory already on hand. Microprocessors are replenished in very large periodic lots ranging from 22,697 units to 98,428 units, with scheduled arrivals fixed at 10 hours. On top of that, PrecisionParts typically delivers with an exponential lead time averaging 18 hours, charges $376.859 per unit, and is paid exactly 74 days after delivery. At the microprocessor stock point, inventory is reviewed every 23 hours, carrying cost runs at $9.125 per unit, and shortages cost $28.671 per unit. Manufacturing happens at the Production Center through an operation called molding, where microprocessors are converted into smartphones in a cycle that varies uniformly between 4 hours and 15 hours, and the completed smartphones are then sent to the Warehouse. Customer demand comes from Bosch Automotive, which places orders for smartphones every 20 hours flat, with order quantities ranging from 2,307,760 units to 4,395,392 units. Delivery to the customer takes 11 hours, and any unmet demand is backordered rather than lost, so orders wait until stock becomes available. Revenue is strong at $2,949.57 per unit, and Bosch Automotive pays anywhere from 22 days to 35 days after receipt. The network is simple and direct: PrecisionParts feeds the Production Center, the Production Center feeds the Warehouse, and the model runs over a horizon of 730 hours with 5 replications.
