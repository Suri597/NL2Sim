This network centers on the Integration Center, which makes two finished electronics products, SoC and FPGA, using carbon_fiber and ceramic_substrate, with finished goods then moving on to the Storage Facility. The product structure is straightforward but very different by item: each SoC consumes 4 units of carbon_fiber and 2 units of ceramic_substrate, while each FPGA uses 2 units of ceramic_substrate. Carbon_fiber is controlled with a min-max style replenishment rule, triggering orders when stock falls below 4658.62 units and topping inventory back up to 7195.86 units; shortage exposure on that material is valued at $31.275 per unit, and it is reviewed every 28 weeks. Ceramic_substrate is sourced on demand, carries a holding cost of $0.887 per unit, and is reviewed every 20 weeks. On the supply side, GlassCore Ltd. provides carbon_fiber at $406.845 per unit, with lead times averaging 10.0 weeks, payment due between 8.0 weeks and 45.0 weeks after receipt, and a maximum delivery size of 735001 units; shipments from GlassCore Ltd. to the Integration Center take between 5.0 weeks and 9.0 weeks. Process Go. is the second carbon_fiber source, charging $393.469 per unit, with a much faster lead time averaging 1.0 weeks, payment terms ranging from 24.0 weeks to 65.0 weeks, and capacity capped at 562853 units per order. PrecisionParts supplies ceramic_substrate at $278.602 per unit, typically delivering in 16.0 weeks to 29.0 weeks, with fixed payment due 23.0 weeks after delivery and a limit of 46163 units per shipment. Manufacturing is split into 2 activities at the Integration Center: an inspection step that turns carbon_fiber and ceramic_substrate into SoC in exactly 26.0 weeks, and a cutting step that converts ceramic_substrate into FPGA in exactly 1.0 weeks. The site manages SoC, carbon_fiber, and ceramic_substrate in one flow, and FPGA with ceramic_substrate in another. There is also a Conveyor Belt resource with capacity for 3 units at a time, service time between 1.727 weeks and 2.501 weeks, and an operating cost of $3.158 per week. Finished inventory starts very high, with 3688714 units of SoC on hand and 4503323 units of FPGA. Holding SoC costs $7.68 per unit, shortages cost $18.226 per unit, and stock is reviewed every 1 week; FPGA holding runs at $5.031 per unit with the same 1 week review cycle. Demand comes from 2 customers. TechCorp buys SoC, placing orders every 58.0 weeks, with demand centered around 712187.0 units and varying by about 139095.0 units. Customer delivery for TechCorp takes exactly 18.0 weeks, the selling price is $1392.001 per unit, and cash is collected between 25.0 weeks and 67.0 weeks later. If SoC inventory is short, whatever is available is shipped and the remainder is lost. Infineon Technologies purchases FPGA, with order arrivals anywhere from 9.0 weeks to 41.0 weeks apart and a fixed demand of 2922087.0 units each time. FPGA orders are delivered in exactly 22.0 weeks, sold at $4112.165 per unit, and paid between 23.0 weeks and 77.0 weeks after shipment; any unfilled quantity is backordered rather than lost. In structural terms, the node setup links GlassCore Ltd., PrecisionParts, and Process Go. to the Integration Center and Storage Facility, and both SoC and FPGA flow from the Integration Center into storage. The model runs over a planning horizon of 100 weeks with 1 replication.
