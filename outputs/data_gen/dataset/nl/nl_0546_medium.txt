This setup revolves around a Fab that turns plastic_pellet and solder_paste into a manufactured sub-component called power_module, and then uses those materials to produce both laptop and SoC units, with finished goods moving on to the Regional Depot. The bill of material is pretty material-intensive: each power_module consumes 3 units of solder_paste and 2 units of plastic_pellet, each laptop requires 4 units of power_module plus 1 unit of plastic_pellet and 1 unit of solder_paste, and each SoC uses 1 unit of power_module together with 4 units of solder_paste. On the raw material side, plastic_pellet is managed with a min-max style replenishment rule that triggers below 3517.61 units and restores stock to 8121.75 units, with a shortage penalty of $7.571 per unit and inventory reviewed every 12 hours. Solder_paste follows a similar approach, reordering when inventory drops under 4519.48 units and topping back up to 6909.37 units, with a much steeper shortage cost of $44.663 per unit and a 2-hour review cycle. Inside the plant, power_module starts with 1104749 units on hand, carries a shortage cost of $30.256 per unit, and is checked every 1 hour. Finished inventory is also substantial, with 3042963 units of laptop and 2007960 units of SoC initially available; their shortage costs are $46.217 per unit and $26.447 per unit respectively, and both are reviewed every 1 hour. Supply comes from three vendors connected into the same network as Fab and Regional Depot.
