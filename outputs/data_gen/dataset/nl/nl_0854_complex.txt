This network revolves around a Production Center feeding a Storage Facility and ultimately serving Sony Electronics, Infineon Technologies, and Schneider Electric over a 365-week planning horizon, with 5 replications in the study. The business starts with 3 raw inputs: carbon_fiber, lithium_cell, and epoxy_compound. From those, the plant builds 2 kinds of pre-assembled parts. A cooling_unit consumes 3 units of epoxy_compound, 3 units of carbon_fiber, and 5 units of lithium_cell, while a circuit_board uses 1 unit of lithium_cell. Those building blocks then support 3 finished lines: each solar_panel needs 2 units of carbon_fiber, 1 unit of epoxy_compound, 4 units of cooling_unit, and 1 unit of circuit_board; each medical_device requires 3 units of lithium_cell and 3 units of circuit_board; and each camera uses 4 units of cooling_unit plus 3 units of circuit_board. On the supply side, BatteryTech provides carbon_fiber to the Production Center with lead times ranging from 1.0 weeks to 5.0 weeks, at a purchase cost of $385.486 per unit, and payment is made between 10.0 days and 30.0 days after receipt. Carbon_fiber is sourced on demand, with a holding cost of $5.84 per unit, a shortage penalty of $27.293 per unit, and stock reviewed every 12 weeks. Process Go. ships lithium_cell to the Production Center with a fixed 19.0-week lead time, charges $494.341 per unit, offers 79.0-day payment terms, and can supply up to 275400 units per delivery. Lithium_cell is replenished in periodic lots averaging 22401.0 units with variability of about 2761.0 units, arriving every 5.0 weeks. ElectroParts supplies epoxy_compound with an exponential lead time averaging 7.0 weeks, a unit cost of $362.633, payment due between 30.0 days and 65.0 days, and a maximum order size of 159116 units. Epoxy_compound follows a min-max style replenishment rule: when stock drops below 3529.63 units, it is raised to 9531.75 units; its shortage cost is $33.229 per unit and it is reviewed every 21 weeks. Inside the operation, the Production Center manages circuit_board, epoxy_compound, cooling_unit, solar_panel, and carbon_fiber for a polishing step that turns carbon_fiber, epoxy_compound, cooling_unit, and circuit_board into solar_panel. That step takes between 8.0 weeks and 22.0 weeks and uses the Welding Station. The Welding Station has capacity for 5 units, runs at a steady 2.41 weeks per job, and costs $4.103 per week to operate. The same Production Center also manages lithium_cell, circuit_board, and medical_device for a welding step that converts lithium_cell and circuit_board into medical_device in exactly 27.0 weeks. A third manufacturing flow at the Production Center manages circuit_board, cooling_unit, and camera, where an inspection step transforms cooling_unit and circuit_board into camera in anywhere from 10.0 weeks to 26.0 weeks. The site also has an Assembly Line with capacity of 3 units, service times averaging 4.905 weeks, and an operating cost of $15.743 per week. Starting inventories are quite large: 1356521 units of cooling_unit, 4741146 units of circuit_board, 632582 units of solar_panel, 2283348 units of medical_device, and 3061947 units of camera. Cooling_unit carries a holding cost of $2.245 per unit, a shortage cost of $37.435 per unit, and is reviewed every 1 week. Circuit_board is also reviewed every 1 week and has a shortage cost of $48.547 per unit. For finished goods, solar_panel begins with 632582 units on hand, has a shortage cost of $17.388 per unit, and is reviewed every 1 week; medical_device starts at 2283348 units, has a holding cost of $1.046 per unit, and is reviewed every 1 week; camera opens with 3061947 units, carries a holding cost of $5.351 per unit, a shortage cost of $40.946 per unit, and is checked every 1 week. Customer demand patterns are very different by account. Sony Electronics buys solar_panel, with orders arriving exponentially at an average of 7.0 weeks, demand centered on 242608.0 units with variability of 42823.0 units, and requested delivery somewhere between 8.0 weeks and 22.0 weeks. Solar panels sell to Sony Electronics at $4419.39 per unit, customers pay in 41.0 days, and if inventory is unavailable the sale is lost. Infineon Technologies orders medical_device on a very regular cadence, between 19.0 weeks and 20.0 weeks, with demand around 3479773.0 units and variation of 246250.0 units. Their delivery expectation ranges from 4.0 weeks to 26.0 weeks, the selling price is $4376.389 per unit, payment comes between 11.0 days and 86.0 days, and the service rule is partial fulfillment with the remainder backordered. Schneider Electric purchases camera, with order arrivals averaging 47.0 weeks, a fixed demand of 2790167.0 units, and a firm customer lead time of 8.0 weeks. Cameras are sold at $2745.159 per unit, payment lands between 13.0 days and 67.0 days, and any unmet demand is lost rather than queued. In terms of physical flow, BatteryTech, Process Go., and ElectroParts all feed the Production Center, and the Production Center sends solar_panel, medical_device, and camera onward to the Storage Facility before customer fulfillment.
