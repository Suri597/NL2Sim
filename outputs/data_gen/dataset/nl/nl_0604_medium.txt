This setup revolves around making 2 finished goods, smartphone and EV_battery, using epoxy_compound and glass_fiber as the only incoming materials. Each smartphone consumes 2 units of epoxy_compound and 1 unit of glass_fiber, while each EV_battery uses 3 units of epoxy_compound and 1 unit of glass_fiber. The network links 3 suppliers: PrecisionParts and FastChip Corp. both feed epoxy_compound into Main Factory, and PolymerSource sends glass_fiber there as well; from Main Factory, both smartphone and EV_battery move on to the Storage Facility. PrecisionParts supplies that material with an exponential lead time averaging 12.0 hours at $237.556 per unit, and payment is due exactly 72.0 days after delivery. FastChip Corp. is the faster but pricier backup for epoxy_compound, with lead time averaging 1.0 hours, a transfer time of 1.0 hours to Main Factory, a unit cost of $459.052, and payment terms ranging from 39.0 to 58.0 days. Glass_fiber, by contrast, arrives in very large scheduled lots of 20363.0 units, delivered every 20.0 hours. Its stock is checked every 9 hours, and shortage carries a penalty of $36.252 per unit. PolymerSource provides it with an exponential lead time averaging 14.0 hours, charges $19.193 per unit, offers exactly 51.0-day payment terms, and can supply up to 491665 units per delivery. The EV_battery line uses a testing step with the same 2 inputs, and each run takes between 7.0 and 24.0 hours. Main Factory manages inventory for smartphone, glass_fiber, and epoxy_compound in one flow, and for EV_battery, glass_fiber, and epoxy_compound in another, while Storage Facility serves as the warehouse downstream.
