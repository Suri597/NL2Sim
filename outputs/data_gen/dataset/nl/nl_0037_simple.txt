This setup revolves around making a medical_device from carbon_fiber, with ChipCo feeding the operation and Schneider Electric buying the finished output. The bill of materials is straightforward: each 1 unit of medical_device requires 1 unit of carbon_fiber. Supply of carbon_fiber is triggered by demand rather than a fixed replenishment schedule, and shortages on that input carry a penalty of $36.792 per unit. The raw material position is reviewed every 10 hours. ChipCo provides the carbon_fiber with a lead time fixed at exactly 3 hours, charges $464.922 per unit, and can supply up to 79803 units per order. On the payment side, ChipCo is paid somewhere between 45 hours and 70 hours after delivery. Manufacturing happens at the Pilot Line, where an inspection step converts carbon_fiber into medical_device in a consistent 15-hour cycle. The Pilot Line manages both the carbon_fiber stock and the finished medical_device inventory, and completed units then move on to the Regional Depot. Finished goods start with 2372366 units of medical_device on hand. Holding that stock costs $5.107 per unit, shortages cost $31.246 per unit, and the finished inventory is reviewed every 1 hour. Customer demand comes from Schneider Electric, with orders arriving every 7 hours for a fixed 2550687 units. Delivery to the customer takes between 2 hours and 18 hours, and the business follows a backorder approach, so any unmet demand waits until stock becomes available. The medical_device sells at $1626.276 per unit, and customer payment comes exactly 75 hours later. The network itself is simple: ChipCo supplies carbon_fiber to the Pilot Line, and the Pilot Line sends medical_device to the Regional Depot. The model runs for a 200-hour horizon with 5 replications.
