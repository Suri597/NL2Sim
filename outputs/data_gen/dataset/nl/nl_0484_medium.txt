This setup revolves around two purchased inputs, copper_wire and resin, which feed a multi-product manufacturing network producing both EV_battery units and smartphone units. A heat_sink is first built up as an internal sub-component using 1 unit of copper_wire and 3 units of resin, and that part is then consumed downstream: each EV_battery requires 1 unit of resin, 1 unit of copper_wire, and 2 units of heat_sink, while each smartphone uses 1 unit of heat_sink and 2 units of resin. Both raw materials are sourced on an as-needed basis. Copper_wire comes from PolymerSource and is shipped to Main Factory with a lead time fixed at 16.0 weeks, at a purchase cost of $471.038 per unit, and payment is made between 44.0 and 79.0 days after delivery. Resin is supplied by MegaSupply to Main Factory, with replenishment arriving somewhere between 11.0 and 21.0 weeks, at $95.161 per unit, on 36.0-day payment terms. Resin inventory also carries a holding charge of $9.06 per unit and is reviewed every 29 weeks. On the shop floor, Main Factory manages heat_sink, resin, EV_battery, and copper_wire inventory, and runs a molding step that takes resin, copper_wire, and heat_sink as inputs to make EV_battery units. That operation runs in a 9.0 to 29.0 week cycle and depends on the Injection Molder resource. The Injection Molder has a capacity of 4 units, a service time fixed at 5.349 weeks, an operating expense of $3.769 per week, and it is not perfectly reliable: it stays up for 388.548 weeks at a time, then goes down for 19.469 weeks. Advanced Fab handles heat_sink, resin, and smartphone stock, using its own molding operation to turn heat_sink and resin into smartphone units in anywhere from 1.0 to 9.0 weeks, with no named resource requirement attached. Starting stock is very large in this model, with 957901 units of heat_sink on hand, 3472668 units of EV_battery, and 3467224 units of smartphone. EV_battery inventory is reviewed every 1 week, with a holding cost of $8.331 per unit and a shortage cost of $7.595 per unit. Smartphone inventory is also reviewed every 1 week, and shortages are much more expensive there, at $38.611 per unit. On the demand side, Siemens Digital buys EV_battery units, placing orders every 17.0 weeks in a uniform 17.0 to 17.0 week pattern, with demand centered around 3889960.0 units and varying by about 7522.0 units. Customer lead time is exactly 17.0 weeks, the selling price is $755.319 per unit, and payment is collected 44.0 days after delivery. If stock runs short, available units ship immediately and the balance is backordered. Samsung Electronics purchases smartphone units with order arrivals averaging 51.0 weeks, demand ranging from 699508.0 to 3881598.0 units, a customer lead time fixed at 15.0 weeks, a selling price of $2985.589 per unit, and customer payment due 77.0 days after receipt; here too, available stock ships first and the remainder is backordered. In terms of network layout, the node structure includes suppliers PolymerSource and MegaSupply along with Main Factory, Advanced Fab, and Storage Facility. Material flows are straightforward: copper_wire moves from PolymerSource to Main Factory, resin moves from MegaSupply to Main Factory, EV_battery moves from Main Factory to Storage Facility, and smartphone moves from Advanced Fab to Storage Facility. The model runs over a 100-week horizon, with results collected after a 50-week warm-up period, across 5 replications.
