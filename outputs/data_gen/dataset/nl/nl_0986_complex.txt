This setup revolves around Plant A making two electronic products, FPGA and SoC, with support from SiliconWorks, ElectroParts, and a downstream Storage Facility. The raw inputs are glass_fiber and gold_wire, and Plant A also builds a fabricated component called lens_assembly before using it in finished goods. Each lens_assembly consumes 4 units of glass_fiber and 4 units of gold_wire. FPGA is a bit more material-intensive, requiring 4 units of gold_wire, 1 unit of glass_fiber, and 1 unit of lens_assembly per unit, while each SoC uses 1 unit of lens_assembly. On the inbound side, SiliconWorks supplies glass_fiber to Plant A with lead times averaging 15.0 hours, at a cost of $44.757 per unit, with payment due somewhere between 11.0 hours and 37.0 hours after receipt, and it can supply up to 137819 units per delivery. Glass_fiber itself is restocked on a regular schedule every 45342.0 hours, with arrivals ranging from 15.0 hours to 28.0 hours, and shortages carry a penalty of $28.228 per unit; this stock is reviewed every 4 hours. ElectroParts provides gold_wire to Plant A with lead times between 3.0 hours and 27.0 hours, charging $340.849 per unit, under payment terms ranging from 39.0 hours to 74.0 hours, and with a maximum order size of 657344 units. Gold_wire follows a min-max style replenishment rule: when inventory drops below 4786.7 units, it is topped back up to 5875.5 units. Holding gold_wire costs $4.313 per unit, and it is checked every 24 hours. Inside the operation, Plant A starts with 4465286 units of lens_assembly on hand, with holding cost at $1.108 per unit and a 1-hour review cycle. Finished goods inventory is also substantial at the outset, with 4596502 units of FPGA and 1831222 units of SoC. FPGA inventory holding runs at $3.244 per unit with review every 1 hour, while SoC is monitored every 1 hour and has a shortage cost of $39.823 per unit. Production at Plant A happens through two manufacturing steps. The bonding operation converts gold_wire, glass_fiber, and lens_assembly into FPGA, runs on the Assembly Line, and takes exactly 21.0 hours per cycle. The Assembly Line has capacity for 1 unit at a time, a consistent 4.806-hour service time, and an operating cost of $1.514 per hour. The testing step turns lens_assembly into SoC in exactly 15.0 hours per cycle. In parallel, there is also a Conveyor Belt resource with capacity of 1 unit, service times averaging 5.075 hours, and an operating cost of $6.318 per hour. Once completed, both FPGA and SoC move from Plant A to the Storage Facility. On the demand side, ABB Industrial buys FPGA, placing orders somewhere between every 28.0 hours and 29.0 hours for a fixed 2770412.0 units at a time. Customer deliveries take 25.0 hours, the selling price is $3206.136 per unit, payment comes between 28.0 hours and 35.0 hours later, and any shortfall is backordered until stock becomes available. Siemens Digital purchases SoC with order arrivals averaging 21.0 hours, each request fixed at 1498553.0 units. SoC shipments take between 11.0 hours and 17.0 hours, sell at $1739.022 per unit, and are paid 27.0 hours after delivery. If SoC inventory is short, Plant A ships whatever is available and loses the rest of the demand. The modeled network explicitly links SiliconWorks and ElectroParts as suppliers, Plant A and the Storage Facility as operating nodes, with raw materials flowing into Plant A and finished FPGA and SoC moving onward to storage. The scenario is evaluated over a 500-hour horizon with 10 replications.
