This network revolves around three inbound materials—steel_rod, plastic_pellet, and solder_paste—feeding an Integration Center that makes both a fabricated sub-component called sensor_array and two sellable items, SoC and industrial_sensor, with finished goods then moving on to the Storage Facility. The sensor_array itself is built from 1 unit of plastic_pellet, 3 units of solder_paste, and 1 unit of steel_rod, while each SoC consumes 3 units of solder_paste plus 3 units of sensor_array. The industrial_sensor is simpler, using 3 units of plastic_pellet and 1 unit of steel_rod. On the sourcing side, MegaSupply provides steel_rod on a demand-triggered basis, with lead times ranging from 11 days to 20 days, a purchase cost of $22.776 per unit, payment due 19 days after delivery, and a maximum order size of 750243 units. Steel_rod carries a shortage penalty of $3.888 per unit and is reviewed every 13 days. ChipCo supplies plastic_pellet as needed as well, with an exponential lead time averaging 22 days, a unit cost of $37.833, and net 15 days payment terms; plastic_pellet has a shortage cost of $10.694 per unit and a 25-day review cadence. FastChip Corp. handles solder_paste, which arrives on a periodic schedule in quantities centered around 53314.0 units with variability of about 15108.0 units, and those deliveries show up somewhere between 11 days and 14 days apart. Its supplier lead time is exactly 8 days, the purchase cost is $277.827 per unit, and payment is made 87 days after receipt. Solder_paste also has a holding cost of $0.65 per unit, a shortage cost of $44.165 per unit, and a review time of 28 days. Inside the operation, the Integration Center manages solder_paste, sensor_array, and SoC in one manufacturing flow and industrial_sensor, plastic_pellet, and steel_rod in another. The testing step converts solder_paste and sensor_array into SoC in a cycle that varies between 7 days and 12 days, and it requires the Test Equipment resource. That Test Equipment has capacity for 1 job at a time, a service time averaging 6.401 days, works in batches of 82 units when batching is enabled, waits up to 7.179 days to form a batch, and costs $11.916 per day to run. The assembly step for industrial_sensor uses plastic_pellet and steel_rod and takes a fixed 11 days per cycle. Inventory is already substantial: sensor_array starts at 799107 units and incurs $3.11 per unit in holding cost with review every 1 day, SoC begins with 4826654 units and carries $0.285 per unit in holding cost plus a shortage cost of $46.917 per unit, also reviewed every 1 day, and industrial_sensor opens with 4744470 units. Customer demand comes from two major accounts. Sony Electronics buys SoC, placing orders with exponential interarrival times averaging 45 days; each order is typically 2485083.0 units with variability of about 468297.0 units. Delivery to Sony takes between 5 days and 9 days, the selling price is $1571.181 per unit, and payment comes in 22 days to 30 days after shipment. If stock is short, available units ship immediately and the remainder is backordered. Dell Technologies purchases industrial_sensor, with orders arriving somewhere between 30 days and 55 days apart and each order fixed at 3800351.0 units. Dell’s delivery lead time is 1 day flat, the sales price is $931.47 per unit, and customer payment lands between 44 days and 68 days later. Here the business follows a lost-sales approach, so if product is unavailable, the missed demand is not recovered. Structurally, MegaSupply, ChipCo, and FastChip Corp. all feed the Integration Center, and the Integration Center sends both SoC and industrial_sensor to the Storage Facility. The model runs for a 100-day horizon, with results collected after a 30-day warm-up period, and includes 10 replications.
