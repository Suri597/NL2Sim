This network revolves around an Assembly Hub fed by Process Go., MegaSupply, PrecisionParts, GlobalComponents, and BatteryTech, with finished goods moving on to a Warehouse over a 100-hour planning horizon with 10 replications. The raw inputs are aluminum_sheet and silicon_wafer, and those materials support both direct assembly and an internal build of circuit_board. Each circuit_board is made from 5 units of silicon_wafer and 3 units of aluminum_sheet. On the finished side, a camera uses 4 units of silicon_wafer, a medical_device uses 1 unit of circuit_board plus 4 units of silicon_wafer and 1 unit of aluminum_sheet, and a laptop uses 2 units of aluminum_sheet together with 1 unit of circuit_board. Aluminum_sheet is managed with a min-max style replenishment rule: when stock falls below 4842.01 units, it is brought back up to 9124.11 units. Holding that material costs $1.224 per unit, and it is reviewed every 5 hours. Silicon_wafer, by contrast, comes in on a regular cycle with shipment quantities ranging from 29685.0 units to 98248.0 units, and those deliveries arrive anywhere from 9.0 hours to 24.0 hours apart. Starting stock is substantial in a few places: 2480327 units of circuit_board, 3231787 units of camera, 1385258 units of medical_device, and 4441838 units of laptop. Camera inventory carries a holding cost of $6.701 per unit and is reviewed every 1 hour, while medical_device inventory costs $2.804 per unit to hold and is also reviewed every 1 hour. Laptop inventory is watched every 1 hour as well, but the key financial penalty there is a shortage cost of $20.785 per unit. Supplier relationships are mixed.
