This setup revolves around two finished goods, tablet and FPGA, built from epoxy_compound and glass_fiber and moved through Memory Star, PolymerSource, the Integration Center, and the Logistics Center over a 100-week planning horizon with 1 replication. The tablet uses 1 unit of glass_fiber and 3 units of epoxy_compound in its build, while the FPGA requires 3 units of epoxy_compound. On the raw material side, epoxy_compound is managed with a min-max style replenishment rule: when stock drops below 4211.07 units, it is brought back up to 13296.76 units. That item also carries a holding cost of $2.474 per unit, a shortage penalty of $17.969 per unit, and is reviewed every 16 weeks. Glass_fiber, by contrast, is sourced on demand rather than through a fixed stock threshold. Memory Star supplies epoxy_compound with an exponential lead time averaging 16.0 weeks, charges $373.358 per unit, offers 10.0-day payment terms, and can supply up to 883260 units per order. PolymerSource provides glass_fiber with an exponential lead time averaging 13.0 weeks, at $5.921 per unit, with payment due 66.0 days after receipt. Manufacturing happens at the Integration Center in two separate flows. For tablet production, the welding step consumes glass_fiber and epoxy_compound and turns them into tablet units in exactly 25.0 weeks. For FPGA production, the bonding step uses epoxy_compound alone and takes between 6.0 and 27.0 weeks. The Integration Center manages inventory for tablet, glass_fiber, and epoxy_compound in one flow, and for FPGA and epoxy_compound in the other, with finished goods then sent on to the Logistics Center. Starting stock is very high for both products, with 1213653 units of tablet on hand initially and 4107253 units of FPGA. FPGA inventory is also explicitly costed at $7.359 per unit held, with a shortage cost of $25.009 per unit and a review cycle of 1 week. Customer demand is split between two major accounts. Schneider Electric buys tablet, placing orders at exponentially spaced intervals averaging 55.0 weeks, with demand centered around 626483.0 units and varying by about 41196.0 units. Delivery to Schneider Electric takes exactly 8.0 weeks, the selling price is $1968.972 per unit, and payment comes anywhere from 38.0 to 54.0 days later. If tablet is unavailable, the sale is lost. Lenovo Group buys FPGA on a steadier pattern, ordering every 44.0 weeks, with demand averaging 280221.0 units and varying by about 77115.0 units. FPGA shipments reach Lenovo Group in exactly 1.0 week, sell at $2980.226 per unit, and are paid 51.0 days after delivery. In this case, unmet demand is backordered rather than lost. The physical network is straightforward: Memory Star sends epoxy_compound to the Integration Center, PolymerSource sends glass_fiber to the Integration Center, and the Integration Center forwards both tablet and FPGA to the Logistics Center.
