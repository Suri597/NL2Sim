This setup revolves around three incoming materials—memory_chip, glass_fiber, and microprocessor—that feed production at Advanced Fab and then move through the Fulfillment Hub before serving customers like Dell Technologies and NXP Semiconductors. One fabricated component, frame_structure, is built from 3 units of glass_fiber, 2 units of memory_chip, and 1 unit of microprocessor. That sub-assembly is then used to make camera, with each camera requiring 3 units of frame_structure plus 1 unit of memory_chip. CPU follows a different path and is produced directly from 2 units of glass_fiber and 3 units of microprocessor. On the inventory side, the business starts with 4,606,088 units of frame_structure, 2,476,718 units of camera, and 3,408,412 units of CPU already on hand. Memory_chip is replenished in periodic lots ranging from 20,883 units to 46,061 units, with scheduled arrivals every 16 hours; it carries a holding cost of $4.59 per unit, a shortage cost of $47.534 per unit, and is reviewed every 27 hours. Glass_fiber is also supplied on a regular cycle, in batches between 41,462 units and 90,425 units, arriving every 24 hours, with holding cost at $4.784 per unit, shortage cost at $46.568 per unit, and review every 15 hours. Microprocessor uses a min-max style policy, triggering replenishment when stock drops below 1,410.38 units and restoring inventory up to 4,175.95 units; its holding cost is $7.113 per unit, shortage cost is $9.198 per unit, and the stock position is checked every 22 hours. Supply comes from MegaSupply, PrecisionParts, and ElectroParts. MegaSupply provides memory_chip with a supplier lead time somewhere between 9 hours and 26 hours, charges $243.495 per unit, offers payment terms between 14 days and 54 days, and can supply up to 106,770 units per order. PrecisionParts ships glass_fiber in exactly 14 hours at a cost of $311.765 per unit, with payment due 86 days after receipt; the transfer from PrecisionParts to Advanced Fab takes between 4 hours and 10 hours. ElectroParts handles microprocessor supply with an exponential lead time averaging 25 hours, a unit cost of $101.838, and fixed payment terms of 46 days. Production is concentrated at Advanced Fab, which manages frame_structure, memory_chip, and camera in one manufacturing flow and handles CPU, microprocessor, and glass_fiber in another. The camera line uses a welding step that converts frame_structure and memory_chip into camera, and that operation runs somewhere between 16 hours and 22 hours per cycle. The CPU line also uses welding, this time combining glass_fiber and microprocessor into CPU on a fixed 20-hour cycle. Finished goods then move from Advanced Fab to the Fulfillment Hub for distribution. Demand for camera comes from Dell Technologies, with orders arriving exponentially at an average of 18 hours; each order is roughly 3,749,796 units with variability of about 389,185 units, customer delivery takes between 5 hours and 28 hours, shortages are backordered, the selling price is $71.847 per unit, and payment is received 77 days later. NXP Semiconductors buys CPU, placing orders with an average interarrival time of 41 hours; demand is a constant 3,036,584 units per order, delivery is completed in 1 hour, unmet demand is lost, the sales price is $1,933.414 per unit, and customer payment comes 85 days after shipment. Camera inventory is reviewed every 1 hour, with holding cost of $2.768 per unit and shortage cost of $32.59 per unit, while CPU inventory is also reviewed every 1 hour and carries a shortage cost of $21.441 per unit. The network itself links MegaSupply, PrecisionParts, and ElectroParts into Advanced Fab, and then sends both camera and CPU onward to the Fulfillment Hub. The model runs for a 200-hour horizon, with results collected after a 30-hour warm-up period.
