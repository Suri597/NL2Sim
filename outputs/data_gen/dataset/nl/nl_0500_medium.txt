This setup revolves around a Fab operation that turns steel_rod, gold_wire, and carbon_fiber into a heat_sink sub-component and then into two finished electronics products, CPU and FPGA, with output flowing on to a Warehouse. The heat_sink itself uses 3 units of steel_rod, 5 units of gold_wire, and 3 units of carbon_fiber. From there, each CPU is molded from 3 units of heat_sink plus 3 units of gold_wire, while each FPGA is assembled from 3 units of carbon_fiber, 4 units of steel_rod, and 2 units of heat_sink. Steel_rod is sourced from NanoMaterials Inc. and follows a min-max replenishment approach: when stock drops below 1039.08 units, it is brought back up to 3272.39 units. That supplier delivers in exactly 15.0 days, charges $185.684 per unit, and is paid 38.0 days after delivery. Steel_rod inventory is reviewed every 11 days, with a holding cost of $0.349 per unit and a shortage cost of $0.089 per unit. Gold_wire comes from MetalFab Inc. on a demand-driven basis, so it is ordered as needed. Lead time runs anywhere from 8.0 days to 19.0 days, the purchase cost is $73.833 per unit, and payment terms range from 51.0 days to 79.0 days. Carbon_fiber is purchased from PolymerSource in scheduled batches averaging 62325.0 units, varying by about 17534.0 units, and those deliveries arrive somewhere between 13.0 days and 23.0 days apart. PolymerSource has a maximum order size of 695489 units, typically delivers with an exponential lead time averaging 27.0 days, charges $276.663 per unit, and is paid 60.0 days after delivery. There is also a 4.0-day transfer from PolymerSource to the Fab. Carbon_fiber stock is checked every 9 days, carrying a holding cost of $6.227 per unit and a shortage penalty of $17.424 per unit. On the production side, the Fab manages heat_sink, CPU, and gold_wire for the molding step, where CPU output is made from heat_sink and gold_wire in a cycle that ranges from 1.0 days to 11.0 days. The same Fab also handles heat_sink, FPGA, carbon_fiber, and steel_rod for assembly, producing FPGA in a fixed 1.0-day cycle. Starting inventory is quite large: 457417 units of heat_sink, 516234 units of CPU, and 4369445 units of FPGA. CPU inventory is reviewed every 1 day and carries a shortage cost of $47.655 per unit, while FPGA inventory is also reviewed every 1 day and has a holding cost of $9.141 per unit. Demand comes from two major customers. Bosch Automotive buys CPU, placing orders every 16.0 days, with demand centered around 3028204.0 units and varying by 636841.0 units. CPU shipments to Bosch take 7.0 days, the selling price is $3053.853 per unit, and customer payments come in between 29.0 days and 39.0 days. If CPU stock is short, available units ship immediately and the remainder is backordered. Broadcom Limited purchases FPGA, with order arrivals consistently at 23.0 days and demand anywhere from 1955361.0 units to 3852210.0 units. Delivery to Broadcom takes between 2.0 days and 11.0 days, the item sells at $1461.909 per unit, and payment is received 54.0 days after delivery. For FPGA, unmet demand is lost if inventory is not available. The network links NanoMaterials Inc., MetalFab Inc., and PolymerSource into the Fab, and then sends both CPU and FPGA from the Fab to the Warehouse. The model runs for a 365-day horizon with 10 replications.
