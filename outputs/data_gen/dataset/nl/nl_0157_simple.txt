This setup revolves around making FPGA units from steel_rod, with GlobalComponents feeding the operation, Assembly Hub doing the work, and Warehouse holding finished stock before ABB Industrial takes delivery. Each FPGA requires 4 units of steel_rod, and the steel_rod supply is sourced on demand rather than on a fixed replenishment schedule. GlobalComponents delivers steel_rod in exactly 1 week, charges $203.771 per unit, offers payment terms somewhere between 40 days and 68 days, and can supply up to 955264 units per order. At the raw material level, steel_rod carries a holding cost of $6.864 per unit, a shortage cost of $32.439 per unit, and its stock position is reviewed every 22 weeks. Production happens at Assembly Hub through a polishing step that takes a consistent 19 weeks, converting steel_rod into FPGA, and both FPGA and steel_rod are managed there. Finished FPGA units then move on to Warehouse. The business starts with 3110529 units of FPGA in inventory, with finished-goods holding cost at $7.567 per unit, shortage cost at $6.957 per unit, and inventory reviewed every 1 week. On the demand side, ABB Industrial buys FPGA with customer orders arriving exponentially with an average of 12 weeks, and each order is roughly 1864601 units on average with variability of about 471373 units. Customer delivery lead time ranges from 11 weeks to 15 weeks, and any unmet demand is backordered rather than lost. FPGA sells at $4358.534 per unit, and customer payments are received between 9 days and 15 days after delivery. The modeled network is simple: GlobalComponents supplies Assembly Hub, and Assembly Hub sends FPGA to Warehouse. The scenario is evaluated over a 200-week horizon, with the first 30 weeks treated as warm-up, across 5 replications.
