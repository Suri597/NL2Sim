This network revolves around two finished goods, laptop and camera, built from copper_wire and microprocessor and moved through Assembly Hub into the Distribution Center over a 365-day planning horizon with 1 replication. The product structure is straightforward but not identical: each laptop consumes 1 unit of microprocessor, while each camera uses 3 units of copper_wire and 3 units of microprocessor. On the inbound side, copper_wire is sourced from both Process Go. and GlassCore Ltd., and microprocessor comes from Memory Star, with all three suppliers connected to Assembly Hub; from there, both laptop and camera flow onward from Assembly Hub to the Distribution Center. Copper_wire is managed with a min-max style replenishment rule that triggers when stock falls below 4203.74 units and brings inventory back up to 13304.51 units. Its shortage penalty is $18.538 per unit, and that stock position is reviewed every 15 days. Process Go. delivers copper_wire in exactly 28.0 days, charges $66.754 per unit, allows payment 77.0 days after delivery, and can supply up to 488427 units per order. GlassCore Ltd. also supplies copper_wire, but with an exponential lead time averaging 14.0 days, a much higher unit cost of $489.494, payment due 18.0 days after receipt, and capacity up to 931703 units. Microprocessor supply works differently, arriving in periodic lots that average 84274.0 units with variability of 13010.0 units, on a delivery schedule that falls anywhere from 8.0 days to 15.0 days. The shortage cost for microprocessor is $9.996 per unit, and reviews happen every 11 days. Memory Star’s replenishment lead time is exponential with a mean of 20.0 days, the purchase cost is $375.063 per unit, payment terms range from 37.0 days to 46.0 days, and each delivery is capped at 376754 units. Manufacturing is concentrated at Assembly Hub, which appears in two production roles. In the assembly step, the hub manages microprocessor and laptop inventory, turning microprocessor into laptop in a cycle that varies uniformly between 1.0 days and 7.0 days. In a separate inspection step, the same Assembly Hub handles microprocessor, camera, and copper_wire, converting copper_wire and microprocessor into camera with a fixed 20.0-day cycle. Finished goods inventory starts very high: 2378500 units of laptop and 3129229 units of camera. Laptop holding cost runs at $8.622 per unit, with a shortage cost of $28.906 per unit and a 1-day review cadence. Camera inventory is reviewed every 1 day as well, with a holding cost of $3.282 per unit. On the demand side, HP Enterprise buys laptop on a steady pattern, placing orders every 51.0 days for 1133352.0 units, with customer delivery fixed at 9.0 days. Laptops sell at $4838.953 per unit, cash is collected 32.0 days later, and if stock is unavailable the sale is simply lost. Samsung Electronics buys camera more frequently, every 20.0 days, with demand fixed at 1200533.0 units per order. Camera shipments reach the customer in between 13.0 days and 28.0 days, the selling price is $4383.041 per unit, and payment comes in 52.0 days after delivery. If camera demand exceeds what is on hand, available units are shipped and the remaining demand is lost.
