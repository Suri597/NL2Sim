This network revolves around 2 raw inputs, memory_chip and plastic_pellet, which feed production of a cooling_unit sub-assembly and then 2 finished lines: tablet and medical_device. The cooling_unit is built from 3 units of plastic_pellet and 2 units of memory_chip, while each tablet requires 1 unit of cooling_unit plus 4 units of memory_chip. The medical_device is simpler, using 4 units of plastic_pellet. On the sourcing side, memory_chip comes from SiliconWorks and is bought on an as-needed basis, with inbound supply taking between 13.0 weeks and 29.0 weeks, at a cost of $132.176 per unit, and supplier payment terms ranging from 30.0 days to 84.0 days after receipt. Plastic_pellet is supplied by MegaSupply under a min-max style replenishment approach: when stock falls below 2089.89 units, it is raised toward 6963.07 units. Deliveries from MegaSupply follow an exponential pattern with an average of 18.0 weeks, the material costs $93.677 per unit, payment is due between 36.0 days and 40.0 days, and each shipment is capped at 976533 units. Plastic_pellet carrying cost runs at $1.687 per unit, with stock reviewed every 18 weeks. Manufacturing is split across 2 plants and supported by 1 warehouse. Advanced Fab manages tablet, memory_chip, and cooling_unit inventory, and it runs a fabrication step that converts cooling_unit and memory_chip into tablet in a cycle ranging from 17.0 weeks to 18.0 weeks. That process depends on the Robot Arm resource, which has capacity for 2 units at a time, works on a fixed 5.948-week service time, incurs an operating cost of $7.323 per week, stays up for a consistent 485.441 weeks, and when it fails, downtime averages 4.182 weeks. Plant A manages plastic_pellet and medical_device stock and performs an inspection-based conversion of plastic_pellet into medical_device, with each run taking exactly 29.0 weeks. Starting inventories are very large in this setup: 2556426 units of cooling_unit, 3607362 units of tablet, and 2530478 units of medical_device. Holding tablet inventory costs $1.68 per unit with a 1-week review cadence, and shortages carry a penalty of $38.532 per unit; cooling_unit holding cost is $4.82 per unit and is also reviewed every 1 week. Customer demand is split between TechCorp and Apple Supply. TechCorp buys tablet, placing orders every 7.0 weeks for exactly 1492455.0 units, expecting delivery in between 8.0 weeks and 21.0 weeks. Tablets sell at $2801.968 per unit, payment comes 9.0 days after delivery, and if stock is short, available units are shipped and the rest of the demand is lost. Apple Supply purchases medical_device with orders arriving exponentially at an average of 10.0 weeks, in quantities ranging from 1157665.0 units to 3529961.0 units. Its delivery promise is a fixed 25.0 weeks, the selling price is $1899.083 per unit, payment arrives 20.0 days after delivery, and it follows the same partial-fill, lost-remainder policy. In terms of physical flow, SiliconWorks and MegaSupply feed the plants, tablet moves from Advanced Fab to the Distribution Center, and medical_device moves from Plant A to the Distribution Center. The model runs for a 100-week horizon, with results collected after a 30-week warm-up period, across 5 replications.
