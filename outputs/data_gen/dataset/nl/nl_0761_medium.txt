This network revolves around two finished goods, GPU and camera, produced through Assembly Hub and then moved into Storage Facility, with sourcing support from MetalFab Inc. and Memory Star. The GPU uses 4 units of copper_wire per unit, while each camera consumes 3 units of microprocessor. Copper_wire is managed with a min-max replenishment approach: when stock falls below 1663.94 units, it is brought back up to 2749.19 units, and shortages on that item carry a cost of $1.921 per unit, with inventory reviewed every 12 weeks. Microprocessor follows a similar replenishment logic, reordered below 405.85 units and restored to 824.39 units, with a much steeper shortage penalty of $46.863 per unit and a 22-week review cycle. MetalFab Inc. supplies copper_wire with a lead time fixed at 23.0 weeks, charging $315.406 per unit, and payment is made between 42.0 weeks and 52.0 weeks after receipt. Memory Star provides microprocessor at $469.294 per unit; its supply timing is exponential with an average of 5.0 weeks, and the inbound transfer to Assembly Hub takes between 3.0 weeks and 11.0 weeks, while payment terms range from 46.0 weeks to 49.0 weeks. At Assembly Hub, the molding step converts copper_wire into GPU in exactly 6.0 weeks per cycle and requires the Injection Molder, which has capacity for 2 units, an exponential service time averaging 2.02 weeks, and an operating cost of $6.259 per week. The same Assembly Hub also runs a cutting process that turns microprocessor into camera in a 13.0-week to 14.0-week cycle without a named resource requirement. Finished inventory starts very high: 2720583 units of GPU and 2986872 units of camera. GPU stock carries a holding cost of $3.851 per unit and a shortage cost of $40.327 per unit, with reviews every 1 week; camera inventory has a holding cost of $0.09 per unit, a shortage cost of $15.864 per unit, and is also reviewed every 1 week. On the demand side, Broadcom Limited buys GPU with order arrivals following an exponential pattern averaging 18.0 weeks, and each order requests between 2855306.0 units and 3019030.0 units. Delivery to that customer takes between 13.0 weeks and 28.0 weeks, the selling price is $3614.734 per unit, and the policy is partial fulfillment—available units ship and the remainder is lost. Broadcom pays in 46.0 weeks. Sony Electronics purchases camera with orders arriving somewhere between 23.0 weeks and 43.0 weeks, each one fixed at 75864.0 units, with a customer lead time of exactly 8.0 weeks. Any shortfall is backordered rather than lost, the sales price is $4512.508 per unit, and payment comes 23.0 weeks after delivery.
