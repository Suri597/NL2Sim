This network makes laptops through an Integration Center supported by a Warehouse, with inbound supply coming from MetalFab Inc., NanoMaterials Inc., Memory Star, PolymerSource, Process Go., PrecisionParts, and GlobalComponents. The raw inputs are carbon_fiber, microprocessor, lithium_cell, and ceramic_substrate. Upstream, the plant also builds 2 key sub-assemblies: a cooling_unit made from 4 units of ceramic_substrate, 4 units of carbon_fiber, 2 units of microprocessor, and 1 unit of lithium_cell, plus a battery_pack built from 3 units of microprocessor, 2 units of lithium_cell, and 5 units of carbon_fiber. The finished laptop then combines 3 units of lithium_cell, 3 units of cooling_unit, 4 units of ceramic_substrate, 1 unit of carbon_fiber, 1 unit of battery_pack, and 3 units of microprocessor. Carbon_fiber is sourced on an as-needed basis, with a holding cost of $9.526 per unit and stock reviewed every 5 weeks. Microprocessor supply is also demand-driven. Lithium_cell is replenished as needed as well, carrying a holding cost of $0.342 per unit, a shortage penalty of $39.239 per unit, and a 21-week review rhythm. Ceramic_substrate follows a min-max style replenishment rule, triggering supply when stock drops below 4722.53 units and restoring inventory to 11787.11 units. Inside the plant, cooling_unit starts with 412751 units on hand, has a shortage cost of $19.369 per unit, and is checked every 1 week. Battery_pack begins at 19568 units, with holding cost of $3.776 per unit, shortage cost of $39.377 per unit, and a 1-week review cycle. Finished laptop inventory starts at 1350374 units, with a shortage cost of $33.011 per unit and review every 1 week. Supplier terms vary quite a bit: MetalFab Inc. sends carbon_fiber to the Integration Center in exactly 14.0 weeks at $147.544 per unit, with payment due 55.0 days after delivery. Process Go. also supplies carbon_fiber to the Integration Center, but with a faster 8.0-week lead time, a much higher cost of $368.367 per unit, payment anywhere from 25.0 to 64.0 days after receipt, and capacity up to 783650 units per order. NanoMaterials Inc. provides microprocessor in a 9.0 to 18.0 week range at $255.465 per unit, offers 25.0-day payment terms, and can supply as many as 273164 units per delivery. PrecisionParts is the alternate microprocessor source, with an exponential lead time averaging 3.0 weeks, a cost of $421.389 per unit, payment ranging from 13.0 to 57.0 days, and an added transfer leg to the Integration Center that takes between 2.0 and 7.0 weeks. Memory Star supplies lithium_cell with an exponential lead time averaging 5.0 weeks at $140.114 per unit, payment somewhere between 12.0 and 64.0 days, and capacity of 682397 units. GlobalComponents is another lithium_cell supplier, slower at an average of 16.0 weeks but far cheaper at $37.287 per unit, with net 17.0 days and a cap of 339522 units. PolymerSource ships ceramic_substrate to the Integration Center in 1.0 to 7.0 weeks, charges $133.084 per unit, requires payment in 25.0 to 34.0 days, and is limited to 71378 units per order. Production is handled at the Integration Center through a testing step that consumes lithium_cell, cooling_unit, ceramic_substrate, carbon_fiber, battery_pack, and microprocessor to produce laptop output. That step takes a fixed 28.0 weeks per cycle and uses the Robot Arm resource. The Robot Arm has capacity for 5 units, works with service times ranging from 1.098 to 9.464 weeks, runs in batches of 79 units, waits up to 6.464 weeks to form a batch, and costs $16.897 per week to operate. Once completed, laptops move from the Integration Center to the Warehouse. On the demand side, Sony Electronics buys laptop units with order arrivals between 15.0 and 57.0 weeks, each order for a constant 195108.0 units, and expects delivery in 4.0 to 21.0 weeks. Sony follows a pure backorder arrangement, so any unmet demand waits, and the selling price is $3466.645 per unit with customer payment collected in 33.0 to 45.0 days. Dell Technologies is much larger and less predictable: order arrivals are exponential with a mean of 44.0 weeks, demand ranges from 2582256.0 to 4600180.0 units, and customer lead time runs from 12.0 to 19.0 weeks. Dell is served under a partial-backorder setup, meaning available units ship immediately and the remainder is backordered, with laptops sold at $280.662 per unit and payment received 8.0 days later. The model runs for a 200-week horizon, with results taken after a 50-week warm-up period, across 5 replications.
