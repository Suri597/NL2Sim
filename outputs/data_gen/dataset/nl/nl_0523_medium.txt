This setup centers on making a product called medical_device, with GlobalComponents, PrecisionParts, and SiliconWorks feeding the operation and ABB Industrial buying the finished output. The network is simple: all 3 suppliers ship into the Processing Unit, and completed medical_device units then move from the Processing Unit into the Storage Facility. There is also an internal built-up component, battery_pack, which is part of the finished item structure. Each battery_pack uses 1 unit of plastic_pellet, 4 units of resin, and 2 units of solder_paste. Then each medical_device is assembled from 3 units of resin, 3 units of plastic_pellet, 3 units of battery_pack, and 1 unit of solder_paste. On the raw material side, plastic_pellet is sourced on an as-needed basis, carries a holding cost of $6.357 per unit, and is reviewed every 25 hours. GlobalComponents supplies plastic_pellet with a lead time somewhere between 14 hours and 18 hours, charges $149.416 per unit, offers payment due 22 days after receipt, and can supply up to 169638 units per order. Solder_paste is handled differently: it arrives in batches averaging 22370.0 units, varying by about 5375.0 units, on a fixed 16.0-hour schedule. Its inventory is reviewed every 28 hours, and shortages cost $35.423 per unit. PrecisionParts provides that material with lead times ranging from 11.0 hours to 28.0 hours, a purchase cost of $403.464 per unit, and payment terms between 42.0 days and 51.0 days; the transfer from PrecisionParts to the Processing Unit takes between 7.0 hours and 10.0 hours. Resin follows a min-max style replenishment rule, triggering when stock drops below 908.81 units and refilling up to 2082.62 units. SiliconWorks supplies resin in exactly 18.0 hours at $179.895 per unit, with supplier payment falling somewhere between 21.0 days and 60.0 days, and a maximum order size of 746746 units. The Processing Unit is the manufacturing site managing battery_pack, solder_paste, resin, plastic_pellet, and medical_device inventory, and its main operation is welding. That welding step consumes resin, plastic_pellet, battery_pack, and solder_paste to produce medical_device, and each run takes a consistent 26.0 hours. Inventory starts quite high for the semi-finished battery_pack at 2628765 units, while finished medical_device begins at 187196 units. For the product itself, shortages cost $4.558 per unit and stock is reviewed every 1 hour. Demand comes from ABB Industrial every 27.0 hours, with each order fixed at 1283850.0 units. Customer lead time is 14.0 hours, and the business follows a partial backorder approach: available units ship immediately and any remaining demand waits for replenishment. The selling price is $4733.474 per unit, and customer payments come in between 14.0 days and 65.0 days. The model runs in hours over a 100-hour horizon, with the first 50 hours treated as warm-up, and it is replicated 5 times.
