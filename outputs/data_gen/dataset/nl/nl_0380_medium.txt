This network makes two sellable items, router units and EV_battery units, using carbon_fiber and lithium_cell inputs, with a fabricated component called display_panel feeding into the router line. Each display_panel consumes 5 units of carbon_fiber and 1 unit of lithium_cell, while each router requires 3 units of carbon_fiber plus 1 unit of display_panel; each EV_battery is built from 2 units of lithium_cell. Carbon_fiber is sourced from MegaSupply in periodic lots averaging 73245.0 units with variability of about 14220.0 units, and those replenishments are scheduled somewhere between 7.0 weeks and 20.0 weeks apart. MegaSupply’s lead time is exponential with a mean of 27.0 weeks, the purchase cost is $307.985 per unit, and payment is made between 24.0 days and 33.0 days after receipt. A stockout of carbon_fiber carries a penalty of $30.873 per unit, with inventory reviewed every 17 weeks. Lithium_cell comes from GlobalComponents in periodic shipments ranging from 3298.0 units to 58159.0 units, arriving on a regular cycle somewhere between 2.0 weeks and 24.0 weeks. GlobalComponents delivers in exactly 14.0 weeks, charges $15.37 per unit, and offers payment terms between 21.0 days and 24.0 days. For lithium_cell, the shortage cost is $14.089 per unit and stock is reviewed every 30 weeks. On hand, the business starts with 4060148 units of display_panel, 75110 units of router, and 1126330 units of EV_battery, with EV_battery carrying a holding cost of $2.41 per unit and a 1 week review cadence. Manufacturing is handled at the Processing Unit. One polishing step converts carbon_fiber and display_panel into router output, and that cycle runs in a uniform 10.0 to 14.0 week range. The same Processing Unit also runs a fabrication process that turns lithium_cell into EV_battery, taking between 11.0 weeks and 15.0 weeks per cycle. Finished router and EV_battery output then moves from the Processing Unit to the Logistics Center, while carbon_fiber and lithium_cell flow in from the two suppliers to the Processing Unit. On the demand side, Samsung Electronics buys router units on a fixed 39.0 week ordering pattern, requesting 2767122.0 units each time. Delivery to Samsung takes between 14.0 weeks and 19.0 weeks, the selling price is $2333.616 per unit, and customer payment comes exactly 54.0 days later. If router inventory is short, available units ship immediately and the remainder is backordered. Broadcom Limited purchases EV_battery on an every-13.0-week cycle, with demand centered around 2005944.0 units and varying by about 368272.0 units. Its delivery time is a consistent 2.0 weeks, the sales price is $1706.566 per unit, and payment is received between 16.0 days and 18.0 days. For EV_battery shortages, whatever is available is shipped and the rest of the demand is lost. The modeled network includes MegaSupply and GlobalComponents feeding the Processing Unit and Logistics Center structure, and the study runs for 730 weeks with results collected after a 30 week warm-up period, using 1 replication.
