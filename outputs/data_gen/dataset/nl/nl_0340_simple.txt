This network is built around making solar_panel units from copper_wire, with FastChip Corp. supplying the raw material, Manufacturing Site doing the welding work, and Fulfillment Hub handling the finished goods flow. Each solar_panel requires 3 units of copper_wire, and the plant starts with 1257958 units of solar_panel in stock. Copper_wire is brought in on a very long fixed replenishment schedule of 52224.0 weeks, with deliveries arriving every 6.0 weeks, and its supply from FastChip Corp. comes with a steady 8.0 weeks of lead time. The purchase cost for copper_wire is $18.333 per unit, supplier payment is due 20.0 days after delivery, and shortages of copper_wire are penalized at $49.023 per unit, with inventory reviewed every 14 weeks. At Manufacturing Site, the welding step converts copper_wire into solar_panel, taking exactly 14.0 weeks per cycle, and finished solar_panel units then move from Manufacturing Site to Fulfillment Hub. On the demand side, Dell Technologies buys solar_panel with order arrivals varying uniformly between 14.0 weeks and 54.0 weeks, and each demand is a constant 2891148.0 units. Customer orders are delivered with a fixed 5.0 weeks of lead time, the selling price is $3748.721 per unit, and payment is collected 55.0 days later. If stock is unavailable, the sale is lost rather than backordered. The model runs for a 200-week horizon, with the first 50 weeks treated as warm-up, and it is evaluated over 10 replications.
