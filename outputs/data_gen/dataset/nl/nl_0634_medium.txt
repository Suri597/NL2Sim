This setup centers on making tablets through a network that includes SiliconWorks, Process Go., the Pilot Line, and a Logistics Center, with NXP Semiconductors as the end customer. Each tablet uses 1 unit of ceramic_substrate and 3 units of silicon_wafer, and all three inventories—tablet, ceramic_substrate, and silicon_wafer—are handled at the Pilot Line before finished tablets move on to the Logistics Center. Ceramic_substrate is managed with a min-max replenishment approach: when stock drops below 654.41 units, it is brought back up to 2948.85 units. That material comes from SiliconWorks with a lead time ranging from 13.0 to 16.0 weeks, at a purchase cost of $29.432 per unit, and supplier payment terms that fall somewhere between 27.0 days and 82.0 days after receipt. Its shortage cost is $0.595 per unit, and the stock position is reviewed every 1 week. Silicon_wafer, supplied by Process Go., is sourced on demand rather than through a fixed reorder trigger. Process Go. delivers in between 15.0 and 20.0 weeks, charges $24.923 per unit, and is paid between 30.0 days and 58.0 days after delivery; the transfer from Process Go. to the Pilot Line itself takes between 4.0 and 8.0 weeks. At the Pilot Line, the testing operation converts ceramic_substrate and silicon_wafer into tablets, with each production cycle taking anywhere from 7.0 to 13.0 weeks. On the finished goods side, tablet inventory starts at 1617096 units, carries a holding cost of $2.066 per unit, and is reviewed every 1 week. Demand from NXP Semiconductors arrives exactly every 19.0 weeks, with order size varying between 1497651.0 units and 3745542.0 units. Customer shipments have a fixed 9.0-week lead time, any unmet demand is backordered, the selling price is $1493.278 per unit, and customer payment comes in 71.0 days after delivery. The model runs for a 500-week horizon with 5 replications.
