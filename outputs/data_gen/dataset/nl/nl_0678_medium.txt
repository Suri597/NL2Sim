This setup revolves around making solar_panel units through a network that links PrecisionParts, ChipCo, and GlassCore Ltd. with Plant A and a downstream Storage Facility. At the material level, circuit_board units are built as an in-house fabricated component using 1 unit of solder_paste and 3 units of glass_fiber, and each finished solar_panel then requires 1 unit of circuit_board, 1 unit of glass_fiber, and 3 units of solder_paste. Plant A manages all four inventories—glass_fiber, solder_paste, circuit_board, and solar_panel—while the finished solar_panel flows onward from Plant A to the Storage Facility. For inbound supply, glass_fiber is replenished on a regular schedule every 5.0 hours, with batch quantities centered around 43134.0 units and varying by about 4776.0 units. PrecisionParts supplies glass_fiber with an exponential lead time averaging 4.0 hours at a cost of $397.874 per unit, and payment is due 23.0 days after delivery. GlassCore Ltd. is the second source for glass_fiber, delivering in anywhere from 4.0 to 10.0 hours at $430.196 per unit with 32.0-day payment terms. Solder_paste, by contrast, is sourced on demand from ChipCo; its supplier lead time also averages 4.0 hours, the purchase cost is $465.658 per unit, payment is made 77.0 days after receipt, and the physical transfer from ChipCo to Plant A takes between 2.0 and 13.0 hours. Inventory economics are quite different across items: glass_fiber carries a holding cost of $6.897 per unit, a shortage cost of $16.397 per unit, and is reviewed every 8 hours; solder_paste has a holding cost of $1.11 per unit, a shortage penalty of $27.915 per unit, and a 2-hour review cycle. The plant starts with 1213797 units of circuit_board on hand, with holding running at $8.809 per unit and review every 1 hour, while solar_panel begins at 3538593 units, with a holding cost of $4.973 per unit, a shortage cost of $14.138 per unit, and the same 1-hour review rhythm. Production at Plant A is done through a welding step that takes the circuit_board, glass_fiber, and solder_paste inputs and turns them into solar_panel, with each cycle ranging from 1.0 to 11.0 hours. On the demand side, HP Enterprise orders solar_panel every 37.0 hours, each order being exactly 3629385.0 units. Customer delivery lead time runs between 16.0 and 29.0 hours, and if stock is short, demand is backordered rather than lost. The selling price is $2786.406 per unit, and HP Enterprise pays 81.0 days after delivery. The model runs for a 500-hour horizon with 1 replication.
