This network revolves around three incoming materials—resin, ceramic_substrate, and steel_rod—feeding a Pilot Line that builds a power_module and then turns that into two sellable items, camera and SoC, before shipping finished goods to the Regional Depot. The power_module is a fabricated component made from 1 unit of steel_rod, 1 unit of ceramic_substrate, and 4 units of resin. From there, each camera uses 1 unit of power_module plus 4 units of resin, while each SoC consumes 1 unit of ceramic_substrate, 4 units of steel_rod, and 1 unit of power_module. Resin is sourced on demand, with FastChip Corp. supplying it in 8.0 to 20.0 days at a cost of $427.744 per unit, then taking another 4.0 days to reach the Pilot Line; FastChip’s invoices are paid in 41.0 to 52.0 days. Resin carrying cost is $6.661 per unit, and its stock is reviewed every 16 days. Ceramic_substrate comes from PolymerSource in scheduled lots of 94659.0 units, with replenishment arrivals occurring between 16.0 and 21.0 days, while supplier lead time is a fixed 23.0 days. The purchase cost is $269.193 per unit, and payment terms run 42.0 to 57.0 days. Steel_rod is also supplied on a regular schedule, in fixed batches of 67494.0 units, arriving every 17.0 days. CarbonSupply Co. delivers with an exponential lead time averaging 1.0 day, charges $104.888 per unit, and allows a fixed 64.0 days for payment; each delivery can supply up to 190711 units, and the transfer from CarbonSupply Co. to the Pilot Line takes 4.0 days. Inside the plant, the Pilot Line manages power_module, resin, and camera for a polishing step that converts power_module and resin into camera in exactly 11.0 days. The same Pilot Line also handles power_module, SoC, ceramic_substrate, and steel_rod for a coating step that turns ceramic_substrate, steel_rod, and power_module into SoC in exactly 4.0 days. Starting stock is substantial: 2209231 units of power_module, 943304 units of camera, and 4664108 units of SoC. Holding cost on power_module is $9.934 per unit, shortage cost is $43.467 per unit, and review happens every 1 day. Camera inventory carries at $3.723 per unit with a shortage penalty of $31.353 per unit and a 1-day review cycle. SoC is held at $3.945 per unit, with shortage cost of $20.759 per unit and the same 1-day review. On the demand side, Schneider Electric buys camera, with customer orders arriving exponentially with a mean of 11.0 days and a fixed order size of 2495301.0 units. Delivery to Schneider Electric takes between 11.0 and 29.0 days, the selling price is $3601.6 per unit, and payment is received 67.0 days later. If camera is unavailable, the sale is lost. Broadcom Limited purchases SoC, with orders showing up on average every 27.0 days and quantities ranging from 2150407.0 to 3108856.0 units. Its requested lead time is between 7.0 and 9.0 days, the product sells at $2589.368 per unit, and cash comes in after 61.0 days. For SoC, available units ship immediately and any remaining demand is backordered. The network links FastChip Corp., PolymerSource, and CarbonSupply Co. to the Pilot Line, and then moves both camera and SoC from the Pilot Line to the Regional Depot. The model runs for 730 days, with results collected after a 30-day warm-up period, across 10 replications.
