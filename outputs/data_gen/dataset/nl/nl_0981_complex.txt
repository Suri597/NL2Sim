This network is set up around building the router, with Main Factory handling production and Fulfillment Hub serving as the downstream warehouse, while PolymerSource, Memory Star, PrecisionParts, and Process Go. feed materials into the plant. The bill of materials is fairly heavy: each motor_assembly consumes 2 units of memory_chip and 3 units of gold_wire, and each router then requires 1 unit of gold_wire, 1 unit of memory_chip, and 2 units of motor_assembly. On the sourcing side, memory_chip is bought on an as-needed basis, reviewed every 23 hours, with a holding cost of $9.522 per unit and a shortage penalty of $34.643 per unit. Gold_wire works differently, coming in periodic lots ranging from 44719.0 units to 99143.0 units, with deliveries arriving somewhere between 14.0 hours and 19.0 hours, a shortage cost of $1.343 per unit, and inventory checked every 19 hours. Supplier relationships are mixed: PolymerSource provides memory_chip in exactly 25.0 hours at $48.206 per unit, allows payment 25.0 days after delivery, and can supply up to 876166 units per order; PrecisionParts is the other memory_chip source, with an exponential lead time averaging 7.0 hours, a much higher cost of $190.616 per unit, and payment due 39.0 days after receipt. For gold_wire, Memory Star delivers in a fixed 26.0 hours at $205.597 per unit, with payment terms ranging from 52.0 days to 55.0 days, while Process Go. offers a faster 3.0 to 10.0 hour lead time, charges $116.203 per unit, gives 32.0-day payment terms, and is capped at 46617 units per delivery. Inside Main Factory, inventory is managed for router, motor_assembly, gold_wire, and memory_chip. There is a starting stock of 3002457 units of motor_assembly with a holding cost of $0.031 per unit and a 1 hour review cycle, plus 3706401 units of router on hand initially with a shortage cost of $48.514 per unit and reviews every 1 hour. The plant runs a welding step that takes gold_wire, memory_chip, and motor_assembly as inputs and turns them into router output in a consistent 23.0 hours, using the Laser Cutter. That Laser Cutter has capacity for 2 units at a time, a fixed service time of 4.167 hours, and an operating cost of $7.853 per hour. There is also a Packaging Machine with capacity of 4 units, a service time varying from 3.813 hours to 6.006 hours, and an operating cost of $0.223 per hour. On the demand side, Ross Associates places router orders every 29.0 to 34.0 hours and always asks for 3394243.0 units, expects delivery in exactly 18.0 hours, and buys at $1347.934 per unit; if stock is short, available units are shipped and the rest is lost, and payment comes between 8.0 days and 60.0 days later. Apple Supply behaves differently, with order arrivals averaging 28.0 hours, demand centered around 859460.0 units with variability of 79566.0 units, delivery promised in 11.0 to 23.0 hours, and a selling price of $4836.667 per unit. For this customer, available router stock ships immediately and the remainder is backordered, with payment received in 10.0 to 17.0 days. The modeled flow is straightforward: memory_chip moves from PolymerSource and PrecisionParts to Main Factory, gold_wire comes from Memory Star and Process Go. to Main Factory, and finished router units move from Main Factory to Fulfillment Hub. The scenario runs for 500 hours, with results collected after a 30 hour warm-up period, across 5 replications.
