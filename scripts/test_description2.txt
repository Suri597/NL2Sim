A manufacturing supply chain produces two products, Laptops and Tablets, each from a single raw material, with a straightforward one-to-one bill of materials for each.
Laptop line: Each finished Laptop requires one silicon chip, making the bill of materials for Laptops straightforward and one-to-one. Silicon chips are replenished with a uniform distribution of 4,000 to 6,000 units. At each procurement event, shipments arrive after a constant delay of one day. The system begins with 0 silicon chip inventory. Laptop inventory starts with an initial stock of 5,000 units. All silicon chips are sourced from the supplier ChipSource Inc., who delivers with a constant lead time of five days. The supplier charges a unit cost of 150 per silicon chip and receives payment 30 days after delivery.
Manufacturing takes place at a facility named Laptop Assembly, designated as a manufacturing site responsible for managing both silicon chip and Laptop inventories. The assembly operation converts one silicon chip into one Laptop with a constant operation cycle time of four days. Finished Laptops are transferred immediately from Laptop Assembly to a second facility, Warehouse North. Therefore, inventory managed for Warehouse North is Laptops, from where customer demand is satisfied.
Customer demand is generated by TechRetail Corp, who orders Laptops at constant interarrival intervals of 20 days. Each order size follows a uniform distribution between 800 and 1,200 units. Delivery to the customer takes a constant 10 days. If inventory is insufficient, sales are lost. Each Laptop is sold at a unit price of 1,200, and customer payments are received 30 days after delivery.
Tablet line: Each finished Tablet requires one touch panel, making the bill of materials for Tablets straightforward and one-to-one. Touch panels are replenished with a uniform distribution of 3,000 to 5,000 units. At each procurement event, shipments arrive after a constant delay of one day. The system begins with 0 touch panel inventory. Tablet inventory starts with an initial stock of 3,000 units. All touch panels are sourced from the supplier GlassWorks Supply, who delivers with a constant lead time of four days. The supplier charges a unit cost of 80 per touch panel and receives payment 30 days after delivery.
Manufacturing takes place at a facility named Tablet Assembly, designated as a manufacturing site responsible for managing both touch panel and Tablet inventories. The assembly operation converts one touch panel into one Tablet with a constant operation cycle time of two days. Finished Tablets are transferred immediately from Tablet Assembly to a second facility, Warehouse South. Therefore, inventory managed for Warehouse South is Tablets, from where customer demand is satisfied.
Customer demand is generated by Gadget World, who orders Tablets at constant interarrival intervals of 15 days. Each order size follows a uniform distribution between 500 and 900 units. Delivery to the customer takes a constant 7 days. If inventory is insufficient, sales are lost. Each Tablet is sold at a unit price of 600, and customer payments are received 30 days after delivery.
The simulation runs with time measured in days over a 500-day horizon, with no warm-up period and ten independent replications. A 99% confidence interval of daily averages of all inventory levels and cash level are required across replications.
