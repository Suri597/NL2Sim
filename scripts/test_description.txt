A simple single-product manufacturing supply chain produces CPUs from a single raw material, a microprocessor. Each finished CPU requires one microprocessor. 
Microprocessors are regularly replenished. At each replenishment, the shipments arrive after a one day from order placement with order size of 60,000 to 80,000 units. The system begins with no microprocessor inventory. The CPU inventory starts with a large initial stock of 3,000,000 units. All microprocessors are sourced from the supplier Process Go., who delivers with exactly seven days from placing the order. The supplier charges a unit cost of 200, and receives payment 30 days after delivery. 
Manufacturing takes place at a facility named Fab. The assembly operation converts one microprocessor into one CPU with a constant operation cycle time of three days. Finished CPUs are transferred immediately from the Fab to a second facility, Warehouse. From there, customer demand is satisfied. 
Customer demand is generated by Ross Associates, who orders CPUs in every 30 days. Each order size is roughly between 1.8 million and 2.2 million units. Delivery to the customer takes 14 days. If inventory is insufficient, customer is lost. Each CPU is sold at a unit price of 500, and customer payments are received 30 days after delivery. The supply chain network therefore consists of one supplier node, two facility nodes (Fab and Warehouse), and directed flows where microprocessors move from the supplier to Fab and CPUs move from Fab to Warehouse. 
The simulation runs over a 500-day horizon, with no warm-up period and ten independent replications.
