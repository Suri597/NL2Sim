A simple single-product manufacturing supply chain produces CPUs from a single raw material, a microprocessor. Each finished CPU requires one microprocessor, making the bill of materials straightforward and one-to-one. 
Microprocessors are replenished with uniform distribution of 60000 to 80000 units. At each procurement event, the shipments arrive after a constant delay of one day. The system begins with 0 microprocessor inventory. The CPU inventory starts with a large initial stock of 3,000,000 units. All microprocessors are sourced from the supplier Process Go., who delivers with a constant lead time of seven days. The supplier charges a unit cost of 200 per microprocessor, and receives payment 30 days after delivery. 
Manufacturing takes place at a facility named Fab, which is designated as a manufacturing site responsible for managing both microprocessor and CPU inventories. The assembly operation converts one microprocessor into one CPU with a constant operation cycle time of three days. Finished CPUs are transferred immediately from the Fab to a second facility, Warehouse. Therefore, inventory managed for warehouse is CPUs from where the customer is demand is satisified. From there, customer demand is satisfied meaning CPU trasnfer from warehouse to customers. 
Customer demand is generated by Ross Associates, who orders CPUs at constant interarrival intervals of 30 days. Each order size follows a uniform distribution between 1.8 million and 2.2 million units. Delivery to the customer takes a constant 14 days. If inventory is insufficient, sales are lost. Each CPU is sold at a unit price of 500, and customer payments are received 30 days after delivery. The supply chain network therefore consists of one supplier node, two facility nodes (Fab and Warehouse), and directed flows where microprocessors move from the supplier to Fab and CPUs move from Fab to Warehouse. 
The simulation runs with time measured in days over a 500-day horizon, with no warm-up period and ten independent replications. A 99% confidence interval of daily averages of all the inventory levels and cash level are required across replications.
