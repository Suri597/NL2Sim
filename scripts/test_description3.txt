A semiconductor manufacturing supply chain produces packaged ICs from two raw materials: silicon wafers and lead frames, requiring one silicon wafer and one lead frame per finished packaged IC.
Silicon wafers are replenished with a uniform distribution of 5,000 to 7,000 units. At each procurement event, shipments arrive after a constant delay of two days. The system begins with 0 silicon wafer inventory. All silicon wafers are sourced from the supplier WaferSource Inc., who delivers with a constant lead time of 10 days. The supplier charges a unit cost of 300 per wafer and receives payment 30 days after delivery.
Lead frames are replenished with a uniform distribution of 4,000 to 6,000 units. At each procurement event, shipments arrive after a constant delay of one day. The system begins with 0 lead frame inventory. All lead frames are sourced from the supplier FrameTech Industries, who delivers with a constant lead time of 6 days. The supplier charges a unit cost of 20 per lead frame and receives payment 30 days after delivery.
Manufacturing takes place at a facility named Wafer Fab, designated as a manufacturing site responsible for managing silicon wafer inventory. The fabrication operation converts one silicon wafer into one die with a constant operation cycle time of 20 days, reflecting the many photolithography and etching steps involved. Finished dies are transferred immediately from Wafer Fab to a second facility, Assembly & Packaging.
Assembly & Packaging is a manufacturing facility responsible for managing die and lead frame inventories. Die starts with 0 initial inventory. Packaged IC starts with 0 initial inventory. The packaging operation converts one die and one lead frame into one packaged IC, with a constant operation cycle time of 3 days, representing wire bonding and encapsulation. Finished packaged ICs are transferred immediately from Assembly & Packaging to a third facility, Warehouse. Therefore, inventory managed for Warehouse is packaged ICs, from where customer demand is satisfied.
Customer demand is generated by Ross Associates, who orders packaged ICs at constant interarrival intervals of 30 days. Each order size follows a uniform distribution between 1.8 million and 2.2 million units. Delivery to the customer takes a constant 14 days. If inventory is insufficient, sales are lost. Each packaged IC is sold at a unit price of 500, and customer payments are received 30 days after delivery.
The simulation runs with time measured in days over a 500-day horizon, with no warm-up period and ten independent replications. Lead frame goes from its supplier to Assembly & packaging not to Wafer Fab.
