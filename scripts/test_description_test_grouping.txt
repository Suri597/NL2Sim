A bicycle maker builds one product, a Commuter Bike, using two intermediate parts: Frames and Wheels. Each Commuter Bike needs 1 frame and 2 wheels. A frame is made from Steel Tubing, and a wheel is made from Steel Tubing and Rubber, 1 unit of rubber per wheel.

Steel Tubing is bought from MetalCo, whose delivery time follows a normal distribution. Rubber is bought from RubberWorks. MetalCo charges $20 per unit and RubberWorks $5 per unit, and both are paid 30 days after delivery.

Steel Tubing is reordered whenever its stock drops below a threshold. Nothing has been decided yet about how rubber is reordered.

The Frame Shop turns steel tubing and rubber into frames and wheels. The Final Assembly plant puts together the bikes. Finished bikes are sent to the Tempe Site, which then ships them to customers.

There are two customers. CycleShop orders frequently, buys each bike at $400, and loses any sale it can't fill. BikeMart orders about once a week, 5 bikes at a time.

Start with 500 units of steel tubing, 300 units of rubber and no frames, wheels or bikes. Run the simulation for 365.
